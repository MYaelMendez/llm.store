# human://root

human://root is the architectural identifier for final human authority. It is not an implemented URL scheme, verified identity provider, legal status or cryptographic credential.

A computer has a named human authority root and a versioned policy defining who may decide what. It may adopt DAO governance for scoped collective decisions. Record the actual human participants or their privacy-preserving identity references, delegation scope, voting/approval threshold, conflicts, expiry, exit and recovery rules before activation. Do not publish private identity documents or live grants.

Human control is exercised through authenticated, separately authorized operations: propose, approve, revoke, inspect, export and retire. The provisioner creates the runtime under a bounded grant; it does not become its owner. A model cannot declare itself root or convert context into authorization.

DAO governance and individual rights must be reconciled in the adopted policy. Technical permissions, repository control, token ownership and legal decision rights are different facts. Implementations must disclose which they enforce.

## Personalization

Purpose, name, language, presentation and chosen capabilities personalize the environment. That configuration remains a proposal until dependencies, provider access, governance and required grants are verified. White-labeling neither transfers credentials nor asserts ownership of external capabilities.

## Regenerative loop

Intend → Compose → Authorize → Act → Verify → Reflect → Renew.

Renewed intent can propose a different context or computer configuration. It cannot expand authority by itself. Measure useful contributions and demonstrated outcomes; do not infer scientific, commercial or governance efficacy from the interface.
