# æ.store Charter · v0.1 · Proposal

This is a proposed technical governance charter. It is not a runtime grant, legal formation instrument, deployed DAO, service agreement or claim of enforced sovereignty.

## 1. Human authority

The authority root is human://root. Agents, models, registry operators and provisioners have no intrinsic authority over a person's computer. A name, domain, scan, installation, model selection or registry entry grants nothing.

A DAO may express collective human governance only through an explicitly adopted, versioned delegation policy naming its human constituencies, decision procedure, scope, expiration and recovery process. A token balance or automated vote alone is not an implicit grant from every affected person. Resolve conflicts between individual and collective control before provisioning; do not promise simultaneous unconditional supremacy.

## 2. Governance accompanies creation

Every computer proposal includes a governance reference and authority policy before provisioning. Governance is part of the artifact's definition, not proof of a formed legal entity or functioning enforcement mechanism. Missing or invalid policy blocks release and provisioning.

## 3. Context is portable; authority is not

Context sharing is explicit and minimized. Imported context is untrusted data. Parent grants are not inherited by child computers, agents or modules. Delegation requires a separate principal-bound grant specifying the action, target, scopes, limits, approved manifest digest/version, validity interval and revocation reference.

## 4. Registration is not execution

Registry listing, publication, review and capability resolution are separate operations from installation, binding and execution. Reviews disclose what was actually inspected and tested. Publication must identify source, version, license, publisher and immutable artifact digest; a signature requires independent trust and verification, not just a signature field.

## 5. Provision without retaining authority

Provisioners receive only the authority necessary for the named provisioning operation. Completion terminates or revokes bootstrap credentials. Ongoing maintenance requires a separate, visible, bounded delegation. No hidden registry administrator, agent, recovery key or maintenance credential may retain user-level runtime authority.

Infrastructure providers may retain documented host-level administrative power. Disclose those residual controls, hosting terms and practical limits before authorization. Do not describe a hosted computer as having absolute technical sovereignty if that has not been established.

## 6. Govern consequential work

Runtime policy must enforce least privilege, resource limits, explicit tool grants and required human review. An approval binds the exact action payload and relevant versions. Material edits, expiry or revoked evidence invalidate it. Non-overridable technical restrictions remain blocking. A public policy file does not itself enforce these rules.

## 7. Preserve exit and recovery

Owners can export their permitted configuration, context, state and evidence in documented formats. Explain limits imposed by providers and third-party licenses. Recovery must verify identity and resolve governance disputes; it must not silently create a permanent operator backdoor. Revocation stops future authorized work where supported and cannot undo effects already completed.

## 8. Return evidence and value

Return contributions, errors and attributable execution evidence to the human. Receipts identify source revision, manifest and policy digests, principal/grant references, provider operation IDs, timestamps and observed outcomes without exposing secrets. Distinguish provider reports, reproduced tests and independent attestations. Failed, partial and unverified work must stay labeled.

## 9. OpenSourceWare stewardship

Publish reusable source and preserve its license and provenance. Third-party capabilities and models are not automatically open source because they appear in the registry. Disclose dependencies, commercial services, data destinations, retention, costs and removal procedures. Personalization changes the workspace's identity and purpose; it cannot forge ownership of third-party work or confer permission.

## 10. Changes and adoption

Version this charter and review changes in public source. Computers remain bound to the exact policy they adopted until their authorized human governance approves migration. No registry update silently rewrites authority or upgrades a running computer.

**Many agents. One human principal. Context may propagate; authority does not.**
